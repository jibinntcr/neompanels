Before You Request a Panel Quotation, Define These Seven Things

A useful panel quotation starts with a clear requirement. When an enquiry contains only a product nickname and a rough quantity, important details may remain open. A little preparation can make the discussion more focused.

1. Describe the application.

What will the panel become? A furniture component, an interior element and a specialist application can involve different requirements. Start with the intended use.

2. Identify the product and grade where known.

Include the specified product family or the wording from your approved material schedule. If selection is still open, say so rather than substituting a similar-sounding name.

3. State dimensions and thickness.

Include the requested panel size and thickness, with units. If a particular tolerance matters, identify it as a requirement for review.

4. Give quantity with a unit.

“One thousand” is incomplete. Clarify whether you mean sheets, square metres or another agreed unit. For repeat sourcing, add the expected frequency.

5. Explain the delivery destination.

Include the city and state, and any known delivery constraints. Commercial discussions become more meaningful when the destination is clear.

6. List the documents you need.

Identify the relevant specification sheet, test report or certificate requirement. A document should apply to the actual product and scope being evaluated.

7. Share the schedule.

State the required date or project stage. A requested date is a starting point for discussion and should be confirmed by the supplier.

Before sending a BOQ or drawing, remove confidential information that is not needed for the enquiry. Ask for clarification whenever a quotation relies on an assumption you have not agreed.
