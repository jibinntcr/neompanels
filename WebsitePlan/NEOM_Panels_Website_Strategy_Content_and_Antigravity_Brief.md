## 1. The strategic decision

NEOM should own the corporate relationship: who manufactures, what the company stands for, the brands it develops, the businesses it wants to work with, and how procurement teams can begin a conversation.

Its brand websites should own detailed product exploration: ranges, grades, finishes, specifications, availability, samples, product enquiries and any purchasing journey.

This makes the corporate website useful to a furniture manufacturer, institutional buyer, architect, distributor or potential business partner without turning it into a crowded product catalogue.

### 1.1 What we know and what remains unconfirmed

| --- | --- | --- |
| Company/brand name: NEOM Panels | Supplied by the owner | Use throughout |

**Important naming rule:** NEOM Panels is the public-facing identity in this brief. Do not invent “NEOM Panels Limited,” “NEOM Group,” subsidiaries, a holding-company structure or a registered office. Confirm whether the factory is operated by the same legal entity. “Parent brand” does not automatically mean “legal holding company.”

### 1.2 Brand positioning

**Positioning statement, internal:**

For businesses and institutions choosing wood-based panels, NEOM Panels aims to be a manufacturing-led partner that connects product brands, material understanding and accountable relationships, with a long-term commitment to improving how materials are sourced, made and used.

**Short company descriptor, proposed public copy:**

NEOM Panels is an Indian panel manufacturing business and the company behind a growing family of panel brands.

Use “growing family” only if additional brands are active or approved. Otherwise use: “NEOM Panels is an Indian panel manufacturing business and the company behind Starwoods.”

**Core proposition:** Manufacturing capability. Material understanding. Long-term relationships.

**Emotional outcome:** Confidence in the company behind the panel.

**Commercial outcome:** More qualified business enquiries, stronger procurement confidence and clearer routes into each product brand.

### 1.3 How NEOM creates value

| Value pillar | What the buyer should understand | Evidence to develop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manufacturing accountability | There is an identifiable company and factory behind the offer | Factory identity, real process photographs, approved operating facts |
| Material clarity | Product claims relate to a named grade, application and document | Current product sheets, test scope, technical contact |
| Business relevance | NEOM understands recurring supply, project specifications and procurement | Enquiry routing, approved supply capabilities, account ownership |
| Responsible growth | Environmental intentions become operational actions and measured progress | Sourcing records, baseline data, approved plans and periodic updates |
| Corporate credibility | People, policies and business information can be verified | Legal identity, leadership biographies, document archive and contact details |

These are proposed operating priorities. The website should make the underlying practices visible as NEOM develops them.

### 1.4 Voice and vocabulary

Write with the clarity of a manufacturer and the care of a long-term business partner. Use specific nouns: panel, batch, grade, factory, application, specification, sourcing, business enquiry.

Prefer “Discuss your requirement,” “Explore our brands,” “View the document” and “Request product information.”

Avoid generic filler such as “revolutionising the future,” “unparalleled excellence,” “ultimate solution” or “one-stop destination.” Avoid unsupported “India’s leading,” “best,” “100% sustainable,” “zero impact,” “government approved,” “world-class factory” and “pan-India delivery.”

NEOM can look premium through restraint, material detail and clear information. It does not need superlatives.

This is a review of publicly available official website content and information architecture. It is not a hands-on audit of competitors’ form completion, mobile speed, accessibility or actual service delivery. Their published claims have not been independently verified. The implications for NEOM are strategic recommendations.

| Reference | Observed on the official site | Implication for NEOM |

### 2.1 The opportunity for NEOM

The recommended position combines four things: a credible company story, identifiable manufacturing, procurement-friendly information and a simple brand handoff. This is a positioning opportunity, not a claim that competitors lack those features.

Five decisions will make the experience distinctive:

1. **Explain the company before the catalogue.** Within the first screen, visitors should understand that NEOM is an Indian panel manufacturing business.
2. **Make the factory tangible.** Use real material edges, production details and approved factory facts.
3. **Design around buying tasks.** Offer a route for recurring supply, a project requirement, institutional procurement, technical information or a distribution partnership.
4. **Make responsibility specific.** Discuss material sourcing, process efficiency, people and measurement separately.
5. **Make brand journeys explicit.** Visitors should know when they are leaving NEOM for Starwoods or another brand.

Do not copy competitor copy, product names, patented technology terminology, certificates, photographs, customer logos or market statistics.

The segments below are **recommended target customers**, inferred from panel applications and procurement structures. They are not a list of NEOM’s existing customers. Actual suitability depends on the confirmed product portfolio, grade, capacity, geography and commercial model.

Architects, interior designers and consultants are influential specifiers and sometimes purchasers. Give them a technical route even when the contract is placed by a manufacturer or contractor.

### 3.2 First commercial focus

Start with **furniture/OEM supply, project procurement and channel partnerships**. Maintain a distinct institutional/public-procurement enquiry route. This recommendation reflects the website’s proposed B2B role; prioritise again when NEOM confirms its actual products and sales capacity.

Government demand may reach NEOM through a furniture supplier or project contractor rather than through direct government purchase. The website should support both routes. GeM is an official public-procurement platform, but an enquiry form is not a replacement for a tender or marketplace process. [S10]

Do not publish government insignia, “approved supplier,” GeM status, tender eligibility or client names without the applicable records and permission.

### 3.3 Buyer journeys

| OEM buyer | Home → Manufacturing → Brands → Enquiry | Supply requirement with approximate volume and destination |
| Project contractor | Industries → relevant segment → Resources → Enquiry | Project brief or BOQ, with deadline if applicable |

### 3.4 What the website should answer

Within 30 seconds, a buyer should know who NEOM is, that it has its own factory, which brands are available to explore, how to submit a requirement, and where to find the supporting information currently published.

Avoid compulsory logins, forced phone-number pop-ups, automatic WhatsApp opening and catalogue gates that prevent first-time evaluation.

### Suggested process illustration, subject to factory confirmation

Material intake → preparation → panel manufacture → finishing → product checks → packing and dispatch.

This is a content structure, not a verified NEOM process. Use only the stages that match the actual factory. Do not illustrate a fibreboard process if NEOM manufactures plywood, or a plywood lay-up if its products use another structure.

### Quality evidence rules

1. Connect every technical claim to the correct product, grade, method and report.
2. Display certificate scope and validity rather than a wall of unexplained logos.
3. Do not treat a supplier certificate as a certificate covering NEOM or its entire portfolio.
4. Do not treat a management-system certification as product-performance certification.
5. Do not turn “tested” into “certified,” or a brand-level statement into a guarantee for every product.
6. Check current BIS standards, amendments, licensing scope and applicable QCO provisions against the specific product before publishing. Official listings and revised-standard pages do not always show identical edition references; never copy an old year by habit. [S16–S17]
7. FSC claims require the relevant chain-of-custody and trademark basis; wood purchased from a certified source does not by itself establish a blanket NEOM product claim. [S18]

This document deliberately assigns no BIS, FSC, ISO, fire, moisture, emissions or durability certification to NEOM.

### 10.6 Measurement discipline

Record absolute data before creating promotional percentages. Keep product families separate when units or processes differ.

- **Energy:** Track kWh and the relevant production denominator. Purchased renewable electricity and on-site generation must not be conflated.
- **Water:** Separate withdrawal, consumption and discharge where measurable. Define the boundary and period.
- **Material yield:** Define input and useful output consistently; explain moisture basis or other material assumptions where relevant.
- **Waste:** State material type and destination. Energy recovery is not the same as recycling.
- **Sourcing:** Define what share is traceable, how it is measured and which source records support it.
- **Training:** Record who was covered, hours and subject. Do not treat attendance as proof of safety outcomes.
- **Tree programmes, if later adopted:** Separate saplings distributed, planted and surviving; document species, location and follow-up. Do not convert a tree count into carbon savings without a valid methodology.

Do not add unlike units to create an overall “green score.” Do not compare periods using changed boundaries without explaining the change.

### 10.7 Environmental claims and visual identity

India’s CCPA greenwashing guidance and ASCI environmental-claims guidance emphasise substantiation and clarity. Broad environmental claims need evidence; an attractive green design does not establish product performance. [S19–S20]

Use forest green and natural materials as the brand identity. Avoid imagery that implies NEOM owns a forest, operates a plantation or has achieved a closed material loop unless that is true and documented.

Safer principle: “We value careful use of resources.”

Evidence-dependent result: “During [PERIOD], [FACILITY] recorded [RESULT], measured using [BASIS].”

Do not publish “eco-friendly panels,” “zero-emission factory,” “carbon negative,” “chemical free,” “100% recyclable,” “deforestation free” or “saves forests” without the exact supporting basis and appropriate scope.

### 10.8 UN branding

Use the SDG names and numbers as a restrained explanatory reference. The recommended launch design uses custom NEOM line illustrations rather than official SDG badges or the colour wheel.

The UN’s communications guidance distinguishes illustrative corporate reporting from commercial use and prohibits implied endorsement. Official commercial/fundraising branding uses require prior written permission under that guidance. Review the exact proposed use before introducing official marks. [S15]

Do not place a UN emblem in the NEOM logo, product packaging graphic or promotional hero. Do not write “UN approved,” “UN certified” or “official SDG partner” without a separate valid basis. If official SDG branding is later used, apply the current UN guidelines and required disclaimer to that use.

## 13. Three launch-ready editorial drafts

These articles use general procurement reasoning and do not make unverified NEOM product claims. The final technical reviewer should check product terminology against the actual catalogue.

### Article 1: Before You Request a Panel Quotation, Define These Seven Things

**Suggested slug:** `/insights/panel-quotation-requirements`

**Summary:** A clearer material requirement gives both the buyer and supplier a better starting point.

A useful panel quotation starts with a clear requirement. When an enquiry contains only a product nickname and a rough quantity, important details may remain open. A little preparation can make the discussion more focused.

**1. Describe the application.** What will the panel become? A furniture component, an interior element and a specialist application can involve different requirements. Start with the intended use.

**2. Identify the product and grade where known.** Include the specified product family or the wording from your approved material schedule. If selection is still open, say so rather than substituting a similar-sounding name.

**3. State dimensions and thickness.** Include the requested panel size and thickness, with units. If a particular tolerance matters, identify it as a requirement for review.

**4. Give quantity with a unit.** “One thousand” is incomplete. Clarify whether you mean sheets, square metres or another agreed unit. For repeat sourcing, add the expected frequency.

**5. Explain the delivery destination.** Include the city and state, and any known delivery constraints. Commercial discussions become more meaningful when the destination is clear.

**6. List the documents you need.** Identify the relevant specification sheet, test report or certificate requirement. A document should apply to the actual product and scope being evaluated.

**7. Share the schedule.** State the required date or project stage. A requested date is a starting point for discussion and should be confirmed by the supplier.

Before sending a BOQ or drawing, remove confidential information that is not needed for the enquiry. Ask for clarification whenever a quotation relies on an assumption you have not agreed.

**CTA:** Preparing a requirement? Share your application, quantity and destination with NEOM Panels.

### Article 2: Choosing a Panel Starts With the Application

**Suggested slug:** `/insights/choose-panels-by-application`

**Summary:** Product names help organise a range. The intended use determines the questions worth asking.

A panel may be hidden inside the finished furniture or space, but its selection deserves a visible place in the planning process. The starting point is the job it needs to perform.

First, describe the use clearly. Where will the material be installed? What loads, fixing methods, finishes or exposure conditions are relevant? Which requirements come from the design, specification or procurement document?

Next, move from the broad product family to the exact grade under consideration. A familiar category name does not establish that every product in the category has identical properties. Product selection should use the relevant technical sheet and supporting evidence.

Be equally careful with descriptive terms. If a document mentions moisture performance, fire performance or emissions, ask what is being measured and which product it covers. These are specific properties, not interchangeable promises. Ask the technical team to explain the method, classification, limitations and application scope where needed.

Processing and installation also belong in the discussion. The people making the finished product should review the manufacturer’s applicable guidance and confirm what their own process requires. A successful selection connects the material with the way it will be handled and used.

For a project enquiry, it helps to bring the specification, intended application and requested documentation together in one brief. If something is unknown, identify it as a question for review.

A clear question is more useful than an assumed answer. It helps the buyer understand what is established, what needs clarification and whether the proposed product should move to the next stage of evaluation.

**CTA:** Explore NEOM’s brand websites or send a product-information enquiry for your application.

### Article 3: What Should a Responsible Materials Claim Tell You?

**Suggested slug:** `/insights/understanding-responsible-materials-claims`

**Summary:** A useful environmental statement explains what it covers and the evidence behind it.

Responsible material decisions need useful information. A short environmental statement can introduce a topic, but it should also help the reader understand the basis of the claim.

Start with scope. Is the statement about a product, a factory, the packaging, a sourcing programme or the entire company? Evidence about one part should not automatically be interpreted as evidence about the whole.

Then ask about the period and method. A reported improvement should explain what was measured and the comparison being used. A percentage without a denominator or baseline can be difficult to interpret.

Certification needs the same care. Check the certificate holder, applicable product or facility, scope and validity. The presence of a familiar mark is not a reason to assume that every product from a business has the same status.

Plans and results should also be distinguishable. An organisation can have worthwhile priorities before it has measured outcomes. Readers should be able to tell whether an activity is proposed, underway or completed.

Indian environmental-claims guidance emphasises truthful, substantiated communication. For buyers, the practical lesson is to ask for a specific explanation rather than rely on a broad label. [S19–S20]

The same discipline benefits a growing manufacturer. Clear information helps a company recognise what it has established and where it needs to improve. It gives customers a more useful basis for discussion and gives future reporting something solid to build on.

Responsible communication is a continuing task. As operations, evidence and measurement improve, the public story should be updated with the same care.

**CTA:** Read NEOM’s responsibility priorities or ask about information relevant to your requirement.

## 15. Visual identity and aesthetic direction

### 15.1 Creative concept: Material intelligence, naturally expressed

## 16. 3D, illustration and animation direction

The visual idea should be built from the material itself. A visitor should remember panels, surfaces and the company’s identity rather than an animation library.

### 16.1 Signature hero: The panel composition

Create a calm arrangement of three or four thin panel forms over a warm ivory background. Use believable wood grain, a subtle green-painted face and natural edge detail appropriate to the confirmed product family.

The arrangement may echo the angles of the NEOM symbol without replacing or distorting the actual logo. Use soft daylight shadows and an architectural studio feel.

**Initial sequence:** The text renders immediately. A lightweight poster is already visible. Panels then settle into position over roughly 1–1.5 seconds. The sequence ends in a stable composition.

**Optional desktop response:** A very small pointer-based perspective shift, disabled for touch devices and reduced-motion preferences. It must not make text move or create ongoing distraction.

**Technical honesty:** Until the product range is confirmed, use abstract solid panel forms. Do not show an exploded plywood core, fibre structure, coating stack or screw-holding demonstration that implies a construction or performance claim.

### 16.2 Useful inner-page visual concepts

| Location | Visual | Interaction | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brands | Material swatch or product image beside the actual brand logo | Small hover emphasis plus equivalent focus state | Make each brand recognisable |
| Manufacturing | Simplified line drawing of the real approved process | Clickable labelled stages if there is enough content | Explain manufacturing |
| Responsibility | Custom drawings for material, energy and people priorities | Short reveal, then static | Explain the framework without invented impact |
| Gallery | Real photographs | Accessible lightbox | Show the business |
| Enquiry | Quiet static panel edge illustration | None required | Keep attention on completion |

### 16.3 Animation system

- Button and link feedback: approximately 120–200 ms.
- Small content reveals: approximately 300–500 ms with modest movement.
- Hero settlement: once, then stable; no continuous spinning panels.
- Content remains visible if animation scripts fail.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; use static hero and immediate content display.
- Do not hijack scrolling, use a custom cursor or require animation completion to access navigation.
- Avoid automatic carousels. Prefer explicit controls or a static composition.
- Avoid leaf-particle storms, globes, glowing AI networks, floating green planets and animated percentage counters.

### 16.4 Implementation options

Choose the simplest implementation that achieves the visual:

1. **Recommended launch default:** Optimised still render with restrained CSS transforms and an accessible static fallback.
2. **If meaningful and performant:** A lightweight browser 3D scene loaded after the main content, with compressed textures and an equivalent poster.
3. **For process diagrams:** SVG or normal HTML/CSS with real labels and content outside the canvas.

Do not make a 3D engine a requirement for every page. Canvas content must never be the only source of the company description, product information or calls to action.

**Asset concept prompt for a designer or rendering workflow:**

> Architectural material study for NEOM Panels: three restrained panel forms arranged to echo the supplied logo’s angular geometry, warm ivory studio background, forest-green accent face, believable timber texture, gentle daylight and precise shadows. No people, no lettering, no certification marks, no forests growing from boards, no sci-fi lighting. Material construction must follow the confirmed product reference. Where no product reference exists, use abstract solid panels without an exploded core. This is a brand illustration, not a photograph of NEOM’s factory or a product performance demonstration.

### 16.5 Performance targets, internal design budgets

Treat these as acceptance targets, not guaranteed results:

- Target LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 ms and CLS at or below 0.1 where field data is available.
- Keep primary content usable before optional 3D or video loads.
- Prefer a hero poster roughly below 250 KB where visual quality permits.
- Aim for an initial home-page transfer around 1.5 MB or less, excluding optional 3D loaded later.
- Test on a mid-range mobile device and a constrained network, not only a developer laptop.
- Measure actual results after implementation. Do not display a performance score in marketing copy.

The Core Web Vitals thresholds above follow Google’s published experience guidance; the file-size budgets are project recommendations. Field assessment uses the relevant percentile and device grouping, and lab checks are useful before sufficient field data exists. [S23]

If the signature effect harms usability, simplify the effect. The enquiry journey takes priority.
